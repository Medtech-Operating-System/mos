# Provenance

*Where MOS content comes from, and what we will not accept. Read this before contributing.*

MOS is open. Anyone can use it commercially, adapt it, or fork it. That only works if every file in
this repository can account for itself — who wrote it, and what right they had to share it.

This is not paperwork. There is no agreement to sign and no legal cost. It is one sign-off line and
a habit of saying where things came from.

## What MOS accepts

**Your own work.** The common case. You wrote it, you can share it.

**Outside material under a licence that permits it.** Permitted, with conditions below. Rare, and
it should stay rare.

**Work you produced with an AI.** Fine, and expected — MOS is a system for running AI against your
own files, so it would be strange to forbid it. You are the author and you are responsible for what
it produced. Read it before you submit it. An AI that reproduced someone else's text is your
problem once your name is on it.

## What MOS does not accept

**Text from copyrighted standards.** ISO, IEC, AAMI and the rest. Citing a clause number is fine.
Pasting clause text is not, whatever the surrounding paraphrase looks like. This is the most common
way good contributors create a real problem, because the text is right there in the document they
are working from.

**Confidential material belonging to an employer, a client or anyone else.** Removing a logo does
not remove an obligation. If a template came out of paid work, the duty of confidence attaches to
the content, not the branding, and it survives de-branding entirely. Where a contributor has a
consulting relationship with the material's origin, that needs clearing before it is contributed —
not after.

**Anything specific to one company.** Nothing company-specific enters this repository. Ever. That
is an architectural rule as well as a provenance one: a skill that carries one company's assumptions
is broken for every other company.

**Material whose origin you cannot state.** "I wrote it" and "I adapted it from X, which is
MIT-licensed" are both acceptable answers. "I do not remember where this came from" is not.

## Every contribution affirms three things

Carried from `CHARTER.md`, and signed off with a single line — the Developer Certificate of Origin,
the same one-line mechanism Linux uses.

1. **I wrote this myself, or I have the right to share it.**
2. **It contains no confidential information** belonging to my employer or anyone else.
3. **It reproduces no text from ISO, IEC, AAMI or other copyrighted standards.** Citing clause
   numbers is fine; pasting clause text is not.

## If outside material is used

Studying how someone else solved a problem is normal and is not a provenance event. Ideas,
structures and workflow sequences are not protected — implementing the same idea in your own words
is how every field advances.

Copying is different, and has three conditions:

1. **The licence must permit it** and must be compatible with MOS — CC BY 4.0 for content, MIT for
   code. Permissive licences are; copyleft and non-commercial licences are not.
2. **The notice travels with the file.** Attribution and licence text stay attached to what was
   copied, wherever it lands in the repository.
3. **It is recorded in the decision log,** with what was taken and from where.

**Where a pattern was learned from an outside source without copying, say so anyway.** It costs a
sentence. An open project's defence against a provenance claim is its record — a complete account
of what was studied and what was written independently is worth far more than an absence of one,
because the absence cannot be checked.

## Skills

Every skill declares a named maintainer, and a skill that cannot state where its content came from
fails review. See `skills/SKILL-FORMAT.md`.

A skill seeded from a contributor's prior professional work carries an extra step: it is de-branded,
generalised past the originating company's specifics, and cleared with whoever holds the
confidentiality obligation. The maintainer confirms that in the pull request or the submission.

## Licences

| What | Licence |
|---|---|
| Content — templates, guides, skill instructions, education | CC BY 4.0 |
| Code and scripts | MIT |
| The MOS name and logo | Trademark, held by the founder |

The content is open and the brand is not, which is what allows a fork while preventing one from
presenting itself as official.

## If you think something here is wrong

Open an issue, or raise it in the community. Say which file and what the problem is. Material with
a genuine provenance defect is removed while it is sorted out, not defended while it is sorted out.
